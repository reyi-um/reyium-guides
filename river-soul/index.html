Wood-burning fireplaces

River Soul has two wood-burning fireplaces, in the main living room and the master bedroom. They're available roughly November through March.

Firewood: A small amount of firewood is stocked indoors near the fireplace. More is on the deck, in the storage rack near the BBQ and living room door.

Lighting: Before lighting, make sure the damper is open. Build your fire with kindling and a few logs. Please don't use lighter fluid or other accelerants.

Safety: Keep the screen closed whenever there's a fire, and never leave it unattended. Burn only firewood, never paper, trash or anything else. Please leave the ashes in the fireplace.

Before you leave or go to bed, please make sure the fire is completely out.

If anything seems off, such as smoke coming into the room, let the fire die down and message us.

Fire & fire pits

Three Rivers is a high fire-risk area. Fire restrictions set by CAL FIRE usually apply from about April through October each year, and during that time charcoal BBQs and wood fires are not allowed. Your home's propane BBQ and propane fire pit are allowed. We fill the propane tanks before check-in so everything is ready to use.

Propane BBQ: turn the propane tank valve to "open," turn the burner dial, and press the igniter. Please double-check that the tank is fully closed after every use to conserve propane and ensure safety.

Propane fire pit: lighting is similar to the BBQ. If you need help, just message us.

Wood fire pit: Allowed only when CAL FIRE restrictions are lifted, usually sometime between about November and March. If the fire restrictions notice is showing at the top of this guide, wood fires are not allowed. We provide a limited amount of firewood. Please keep fires small and attended, and make sure they are fully out before you leave or go to bed.

Please never discard cigarettes, matches, charcoal or other burning material outdoors, and stay with the BBQ and fire pit whenever they are lit.

Inside the home, candles, incense and other open flames are not permitted, other than fires in the wood-burning fireplaces when they're available.

Trash bins

Wildlife is common in Three Rivers, and bears are strongly attracted to food and trash. Keeping the bins secured protects the neighborhood and keeps wildlife from becoming dependent on human food. The bins are kept on the left side of the home, just inside the gate, lined up along the fence line.

Grey bin: trash and food waste. Put all food scraps, food packaging and anything with a smell here.

Blue bin: recycling. Bottles, cans, cartons, glass, clean cardboard and paper only.

Green bin: yard waste. Leaves, grass clippings and other garden waste only.

Bear safety: These are bear-resistant cans, so please make sure the lids are always closed and latched. Please don't move the bins to the street. Our team puts them out on Tuesdays and Wednesdays and brings them back.

Have more trash than fits? Message us and we'll arrange to use a neighboring home's bins.

Wildlife

Keep food and trash secured to avoid attracting bears. Enjoy wildlife from a distance, and never feed or approach animals. Bears are common here, so please follow the bear safety notes in your home's trash bin instructions. You may see squirrels, deer, black bears, mountain lions and many birds, including western tanagers, violet-green swallows, western bluebirds and pileated woodpeckers.

Swimming & river access

River time is always a highlight. River levels and temperatures change with the season, so check conditions before you go in, and keep a close eye on children. Wear water shoes and any safety equipment you need, such as life jackets. There is no lifeguard, and swimming is at your own risk.

Looking for another spot? Locals also swim at a spot inside Sequoia National Park, near the visitor center. Ask us for directions.

River & outdoor safety

Please use care around the river, rocks, trails, steps, decks and rooftops. River conditions can change dramatically through the year, rocks can be extremely slippery, and there is no lifeguard. Water shoes or secure footwear are strongly recommended. Please use non-glass drinkware near the river and outdoor areas, supervise children closely, and never enter the water when it looks swift, deep or unsafe.

Hapé ceremony

A gathering at the river's edge, about an hour long, with a grounding meditation and the rhythm of the drum. Hapé is a traditional Indigenous Amazonian snuff made from ground tobacco and other plants, offered with reverence and intention.

If you like, you may bring a journal and a small offering for the altar (a crystal, a feather or any token of gratitude). Comfortable clothing is best, and many people prefer to fast for about three hours beforehand. One person is enough to book.

Participation is always optional. If you have any health concerns or questions, please ask us beforehand.

Around Three Rivers

Go explore.

Three Rivers is the gateway community to Sequoia National Park, with restaurants, coffee shops, river access, hiking and beautiful scenery year-round. Road, weather, river and park conditions change with the seasons, so please check current conditions (nps.gov/seki for the park) before heading into the park or planning outdoor activities.

Times are approximate drives from the home. Tap a place for details, or the arrow for directions. Check nps.gov/seki before heading into the park.

Sequoia & Kings Canyon National ParksParks · 10–15 min

Giant sequoias, canyon views and hiking. The Ash Mountain park entrance is about 10 to 15 minutes from the home (travel times vary). Please check nps.gov/seki for closures and current conditions before you go.

General Sherman Tree & Giant ForestParks · About 1 hr

The world's largest tree by volume, in a grove of giant sequoias in Sequoia National Park. Allow about an hour to drive up from the home, plus time to walk the trail. Please check nps.gov/seki before you go.

Moro Rock & Crescent MeadowParks · About 1 hr

A granite dome with steps up to sweeping views of the Great Western Divide, and a peaceful meadow walk nearby, both in Giant Forest. The road generally opens by the Wednesday before Memorial Day and closes once snow makes it impassable. From about late May to early September, it's closed to private vehicles on weekends and holidays during the day, and a free shuttle runs from Giant Forest Museum. Please check nps.gov/seki before you go.

Hospital RockParks · 15–20 min past the park gate

A picnic spot by the river with Native American pictographs, about 15 to 20 minutes past the park entrance and the Foothills Visitor Center at Ash Mountain. A peaceful first stop on your way into the park.

Lake KaweahParks · 10–15 min

A reservoir about 10 to 15 minutes from town toward Visalia, which you pass on the drive into Three Rivers. Good for fishing, picnics and birdwatching. Water levels and fishing rules vary, so please check before you go.

Park visitor centersParks · 10–15 min

Foothills Visitor Center, just inside the park entrance near Three Rivers, is a good first stop for maps and trail advice. Lodgepole Visitor Center is deeper in the park, near the giant sequoias. Please check nps.gov/seki for current hours and details.

Kings Canyon National ParkParks · A full day

Sequoia's neighbor to the north, with the giant sequoias of Grant Grove and the deep Kings Canyon. It's a longer drive from Three Rivers, so many guests make it a full day. Please check nps.gov/seki before you go.

Mineral KingParks · Seasonal, May to Oct

A remote alpine valley at the end of a narrow, winding, partly unpaved road, with trails and wildflowers. The road is open seasonally, generally from the Wednesday before Memorial Day to the last Wednesday in October. RVs and trailers aren't recommended, and weekday construction closures are planned through 2027. Please check nps.gov/seki before planning a visit.

Silver City Mountain ResortParks · Seasonal

A seasonal mountain resort on Mineral King Road, about 3 miles before the Mineral King Ranger Station, known for its pie. It's open in the warmer months, so please check before you make the trip.

Fishing

A California fishing license is required for anyone 16 and older. Short-term and annual licenses are available. You can buy one online from California Fish and Wildlife before you arrive. Rules and seasons vary by water, so please check current regulations. The river is low and slow much of the year and isn't a dependable fishing spot. Lake Kaweah nearby has bass, catfish, crappie and trout. Please take all line and hooks with you.
